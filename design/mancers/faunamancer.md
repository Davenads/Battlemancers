# Faunamancer — Full Design Document

---

## 1. Tactical Identity

The Faunamancer is the only Mancer that fights as a distributed system. Where every other Mancer is a single casting unit supported by infantry, the Faunamancer is a command hub for a small pack of beast companions — semi-independent units that project threat across multiple map locations simultaneously. The Faunamancer herself is a moderate-power caster, but her true value is the action amplification her beasts provide: each AP she spends commanding a companion is an attack, move, or ability delivered from a different location on the board than where she stands. A Faunamancer controlling two beasts effectively triples her board presence within a single activation.

This makes the Faunamancer uniquely challenging to counter. Single-target focus fire on the Faunamancer herself is less effective than against most Mancers — killing the handler does not undo turns of companion positioning. Beast companions act on the Faunamancer's initiative and remain active until killed, maintaining pressure even if the Faunamancer is stunned or silenced (companions do not cast spells; they are unaffected by silence). Positioning the Faunamancer for pack coordination — ensuring the Pack Tactics bonus fires by aligning beast and Faunamancer attacks on the same target — is the primary skill expression. A Faunamancer who commands beasts thoughtlessly, sending them to separate objectives without coordinating attacks, produces far less output than one who cycles beast and handler attacks on a single priority target every turn.

**Primary win condition:** The Faunamancer wins by overwhelming the opponent's ability to address simultaneous threats. One beast advancing on a ranged unit forces the opponent to protect it; the Faunamancer and a second beast targeting the opponent's Mancer simultaneously delivers +25% Pack Tactics damage to both attacks. The opponent cannot respond to all threats in one activation. Once a beast reaches the backline, it either forces a reactive activation (opponent burns AP defending) or kills an unprotected unit. Sustained pack pressure, not burst, is the win condition.

**Core weakness:** The Faunamancer is expensive in activation budget terms. She costs 100 pts to activate, and commanding beasts consumes her AP — an activation spent primarily on beast commands is an activation where the Faunamancer herself cast nothing and moved minimally. Teams facing the Faunamancer should target the beasts before the Faunamancer: each dead beast is a permanent reduction in Faunamancer output, and beasts have lower HP than Mancers. A Pyromancer or AoE Mancer that can sweep beasts off the board with wide fire coverage dramatically reduces the Faunamancer's threat density. Additionally, beasts are not on the activation budget — commanding them costs the Faunamancer's AP within her own activation — but they are also not protecting the Faunamancer physically. She remains a moderately low-HP Mancer who needs infantry screening.

Or she can spend 4 AP commanding two beasts in detail and 2 AP moving to reposition. The 6 AP budget must always account for both her own movement/spells and beast commands.

## 3. Companion System — Beast Companions

### How Beasts Work

Beast companions are a separate unit category unique to the Faunamancer. They are **not** Chaff or Ranged units and do not consume the player's activation budget. They are not purchased during warband construction — they are generated by the Faunamancer's spells and upgrade path.

**Companion activation rule:** Beast companions do not activate independently. They act only when the Faunamancer spends AP to command them during her activation. Commanding a beast costs **1 AP per action** (move 1 action, or attack 1 action, or use ability 1 action). A beast commanded to move and attack costs 2 AP total. Beasts cannot take more than 2 actions per Faunamancer activation regardless of AP spent.

**Companion limits:** The Faunamancer starts with 1 beast companion active (summoned at match start, before turn 1). She can have up to **2 beast companions** active simultaneously at base. The Skeleton Crew upgrade path analogue (see Section 5) increases this to 3.

**Companion persistence:** Beasts remain on the board between activations. They hold position, maintain passive abilities (Scout Wolf's vision range, Siege Boar's blocking presence), and cannot be accidentally activated by the opponent. They can be targeted and killed by enemy attacks — they are real units with HP, positioned on the board.

**On beast death:** A dead beast is gone until the Faunamancer summons a replacement via Call of the Wild (see Section 3, Spell 4). The Faunamancer begins each match with her starting companion; additional companions or replacements are summoned mid-match. There is no "respawn" mechanic outside Call of the Wild.

**Verdant Pact affinity:** All beasts count as NATURAL terrain affinity units for Verdant Pact Terrain Bond purposes. Verdant Pact Terrain Bond grants +1 movement and passive regen to Verdant Pact non-Mancer units on natural tiles — beasts standing on VINES, earth/mud, forest tiles, or other natural terrain trigger their own version of this bonus. This is not a full faction trait for beasts (they are not Pact units) but is explicitly ruled to apply because beasts are natural creatures: beast companions of a Verdant Pact Faunamancer gain +1 movement on natural tiles only.

### Beast Stat Blocks

#### Beast 1: Scout Wolf

| Stat | Value |
|---|---|
| **HP** | 35 |
| **Move Range** | 5 tiles (fast) |
| **Armor** | 0 |
| **Attack** | Bite — melee, 10 damage, 1-tile range |
| **Primary Ability** | Track — reveals all enemy units within 4 tiles of the Scout Wolf's position (removes Fog of War and STEALTH within range); passive, always active |
| **Secondary Ability** | Sprint (1 AP command) — Scout Wolf moves up to 7 tiles this action instead of 5 (one-use sprint; recharges each Faunamancer activation) |

**Role:** The Scout Wolf is the Faunamancer's primary harassment and information unit. Fast, fragile, and useful even when not attacking — simply positioning the Scout Wolf near enemy formations reveals their composition and movement to the Faunamancer player. The Track ability removes the opponent's ability to use cover or concealment near the Wolf, making it a mobile vision ward that the opponent must spend AP to remove. Its low armor means it dies quickly to focused fire; it is expendable but high-value while alive.

**Pack Tactics interaction:** A Scout Wolf attacking the same unit as the Faunamancer on the same turn both deal +25% damage. Scout Wolf's 10-damage bite becomes 12.5 (13 rounded). The Faunamancer's spell damage also increases +25% against that target. Because the Scout Wolf can reach targets 5 tiles away and sprint 7, it can coordinate attacks with the Faunamancer across a significant distance.

#### Beast 2: Siege Boar

| Stat | Value |
|---|---|
| **HP** | 65 |
| **Move Range** | 3 tiles (slow) |
| **Armor** | 2 |
| **Attack** | Gore — melee, 16 damage, 1-tile range |
| **Primary Ability** | Boar Wall — Siege Boar occupies its tile and adjacent tiles as blocking terrain. Units cannot move through the Siege Boar's tile; units pushed by displacement spells (Tidal Surge, Gravimancer) are stopped by the Boar if they would pass through its tile |
| **Secondary Ability** | Charge (2 AP command) — Siege Boar moves up to 5 tiles in a straight line; the first unit in its path takes 20 damage and is knocked back 2 tiles. If the knocked-back unit collides with a wall or another unit, both take 8 collision damage |

**Role:** The Siege Boar is the Faunamancer's tank and disruption unit. High HP and armor make it resistant to focused small-arms fire, and Boar Wall makes it a physical obstacle — enemies routing around the Siege Boar lose movement efficiency, and a Siege Boar positioned in a chokepoint is a 65-HP obstacle that costs the opponent AP to destroy before advancing. The Charge ability delivers the highest single-hit damage from any Faunamancer companion and produces knockback displacement — knocking a unit into a wall or secondary unit for collision damage. Charge requires 2 AP to command, which is the highest cost action the Faunamancer can spend on any companion.

**Pack Tactics interaction:** Siege Boar's 16-damage Gore becomes 20 when Pack Tactics fires. When the Siege Boar Charges (2 AP, 20 damage) with Pack Tactics active, the charge hits for 25 damage — plus collision damage if applicable. A Faunamancer spell + Siege Boar Charge on the same target in one activation deals (Faunamancer spell at +25%) + 25 damage from Charge — one of the highest combined single-activation damage outputs available to the Faunamancer.

#### Beast 3: Verdant Serpent

| Stat | Value |
|---|---|
| **HP** | 45 |
| **Move Range** | 4 tiles (medium) |
| **Armor** | 0 |
| **Attack** | Venom Bite — melee, 12 damage + applies 1 stack of `POISONED` (3 HP/turn; 3-turn duration) |
| **Primary Ability** | Serpentine — Verdant Serpent ignores movement cost penalties from VINES, OVERGROWTH, and MUD terrain (it slithers through undergrowth naturally) |
| **Secondary Ability** | Venom Spit (1 AP command, 3-tile range) — ranged attack; 8 damage + 2 stacks of `POISONED`; single target; cooldown: 1 Faunamancer activation |

**Role:** The Verdant Serpent is the Faunamancer's sustained DPS and poison delivery companion. Its Venom Bite and Venom Spit both apply POISONED stacks, making it an effective Toximancer-adjacent damage escalator. A Verdant Serpent cycling Venom Spit every activation and following up with Venom Bite can apply 3 POISONED stacks to a single target over two turns — 9 HP/turn DoT — without the Faunamancer spending any spell AP directly on poison. Against already-POISONED targets from Toximancer or Floramancer, additional Serpent stacks accelerate toward the DEBILITATED threshold. Serpentine means the Verdant Serpent can advance through Floramancer VINES zones without movement penalty — a significant mobility advantage when the Floramancer has seeded the approach corridor.

**Pack Tactics interaction:** Verdant Serpent's 12-damage Venom Bite becomes 15 with Pack Tactics. The POISONED stack still applies at 1 stack regardless of Pack Tactics multiplier (damage multiplied; status application is not). The Venom Spit at +25% deals 10 damage instead of 8, with POISONED stacks unaffected.

### Pack Tactics Passive (Inherent — No Upgrade Required)

**Pack Tactics:** When the Faunamancer and at least one beast companion target the same unit on the same activation, **all attacks and spells against that target this activation deal +25% damage** — from both the Faunamancer and the beast(s). Pack Tactics applies to each unit participating in the coordinated attack independently (each deal +25%, not a shared bonus). If two beasts and the Faunamancer all target the same unit, all three deal +25%.

**Ruling — simultaneous targeting required:** Pack Tactics requires the Faunamancer and at least one beast to target the same unit within the same activation window. A beast attacking on turn N without the Faunamancer also attacking does not trigger Pack Tactics. The Faunamancer commanding a beast (spending AP) and casting a spell on the same target in the same activation triggers Pack Tactics for both attacks.

## 4. Base Spell Kit

The Faunamancer's four base spells cover distinct combat functions:
- **Bestial Mark** — single-target debuff that amplifies all beast attacks against the marked unit
- **Pack Howl** — aura effect that boosts beast companion stats temporarily
- **Predator's Sense** — defensive/information spell; reveals hidden threats and grants evasion
- **Call of the Wild** — summon spell; deploys a beast companion mid-match

---

### Spell 1: Bestial Mark

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| **Name** | Bestial Mark |
| **AP Cost** | 1 AP |
| **Cooldown** | 0 (usable every activation) |
| **Targeting Type** | Single Target (targeted status; LOS required) |
| **Range** | 5 tiles |
| **AoE Radius** | N/A |
| **Base Damage** | 0 (status application; no direct damage) |
| **Element** | Nature/Beast |
| **Effects Applied** | Applies `BESTIAL_MARK` status to target. While BESTIAL_MARK is active: all beast companion attacks against the marked target deal +4 bonus damage (flat, added after Pack Tactics calculation). Duration: 3 turns. Bestial Mark can be refreshed (recasting resets duration). Multiple Bestial Marks do not stack — a second cast simply refreshes. |
| **Terrain Interaction** | None; purely a unit-status application. Applying Bestial Mark to a unit on VINES terrain: no terrain interaction, but the ROOTED unit cannot flee the approaching beasts. **Design note:** Bestial Mark is the Faunamancer's cheapest AP spend and the ideal activation opener — 1 AP marks the highest-priority target, then the remaining 5 AP is spent on movement, beast commands, and one additional spell. At +4 damage per beast attack, a Siege Boar Gore against a marked target deals 16 + 4 = 20 base (before Pack Tactics), and a Pack Tactics Siege Boar Gore against a marked target deals 16 × 1.25 + 4 = 24 damage. The flat +4 stacks additively with Pack Tactics' multiplicative bonus.

### Spell 2: Pack Howl

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| **Name** | Pack Howl |
| **AP Cost** | 2 AP |
| **Cooldown** | 2 turns |
| **Targeting Type** | Self-centered AoE — affects all active beast companions regardless of distance |
| **Range** | N/A (affects all active companions unconditionally) |
| **AoE Radius** | Global (all companions on board) |
| **Base Damage** | 0 (buff application; no damage) |
| **Element** | Nature/Beast |
| **Effects Applied** | All active beast companions gain `PACK_HOWL` buff: +3 damage to all attacks; +1 Move Range; duration 2 turns. The Faunamancer herself also gains PACK_BONUS as listed in status-effects.md: +2 attack per adjacent companion (inherent status, always present; Pack Howl does not amplify this further). |
| **Terrain Interaction** | None; buff applies globally. PACK_HOWL-buffed companions on NATURAL tiles (if Verdant Pact faction): also gain the +1 movement from Terrain Bond (total +2 movement on natural tiles for those 2 turns — the Terrain Bond movement stacks with Pack Howl's movement bonus). |

**Design note:** Pack Howl converts a moderate activation into a multiplier for everything the beasts do in the following 2 turns. The 2-AP cost and 2-turn cooldown mean it cannot be used every activation — it should be timed for when the beasts are in position to make multiple attacks, maximizing the turns where the damage and movement bonus applies. Pack Howl + Bestial Mark + Pack Tactics on the same target is the Faunamancer's highest single-target damage output: (damage × 1.25 + 4 flat + 3 flat) from each beast, simultaneously.

### Spell 3: Predator's Sense

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| **Name** | Predator's Sense |
| **AP Cost** | 1 AP |
| **Cooldown** | 2 turns |
| **Targeting Type** | Self / Ally target (cast on Faunamancer or one beast companion within 4 tiles) |
| **Range** | 4 tiles (for companion targeting; self-cast has no range limit) |
| **AoE Radius** | N/A |
| **Base Damage** | 0 |
| **Element** | Nature/Beast |
| **Effects Applied** | The targeted unit (Faunamancer or companion) gains Predator's Sense for 2 turns: gains enhanced vision (reveals all STEALTHED, HIDDEN, and CONCEALED enemy units within 5 tiles of the target); all incoming ranged attacks against the target have a 20% miss chance added (instinctive evasion); the target ignores terrain-based concealment (can target units in OVERGROWTH, STEAM_CLOUD, or behind cover as if they were in the open) within 5 tiles. |
| **Terrain Interaction** | Predator's Sense allows the target to effectively ignore BLINDED status from STEAM_CLOUD for the duration — their beast instincts override sensory blindness. If the target is a Scout Wolf, the Scout Wolf's existing Track ability plus Predator's Sense creates a dual-layer detection: all enemies within 4 tiles are visible (Track) AND STEALTHED/CONCEALED units within 5 tiles are also visible, and ranged attacks have 20% miss chance. |

**Design note:** Predator's Sense is a 1-AP utility spell that serves multiple functions — it can protect the Scout Wolf from ranged units that would otherwise target it safely from concealment, or it can be cast on the Faunamancer herself to allow attacking into STEAM_CLOUD zones that would otherwise blind her. The miss-chance component is reactive and defensive: it does not prevent damage but meaningfully reduces consistent chip-fire from enemy ranged units against a key asset. At 1 AP, it is cheap enough to slot into any activation without sacrificing significant spell or command budget.

### Spell 4: Call of the Wild

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| **Name** | Call of the Wild |
| **AP Cost** | 3 AP |
| **Cooldown** | 3 turns |
| **Targeting Type** | Ground Target — summons a beast companion on target tile |
| **Range** | 3 tiles (summon must appear adjacent to the Faunamancer or within 3 tiles) |
| **AoE Radius** | N/A (summon; single tile) |
| **Base Damage** | 0 (summon spell) |
| **Element** | Nature/Beast |
| **Effects Applied** | Summons a beast companion of the player's choice (Scout Wolf, Siege Boar, or Verdant Serpent) on the target tile. The summoned beast is active from the next Faunamancer activation. If the companion limit (2 at base; 3 with upgrade) is already reached, Call of the Wild cannot be cast — the companion slot must be open. Replacing a dead companion: if a beast has been killed, Call of the Wild can refill that slot (the cooldown begins immediately after the previous beast dies, effectively penalizing repeated beast loss). |
| **Terrain Interaction** | Cannot summon onto OBSIDIAN, FLOODED, or ON_FIRE terrain (the beast refuses to appear in impassable or actively harmful terrain). Summoning onto VINES terrain: the summoned beast appears ROOTED (1 turn) — the vines catch the newly materialized beast briefly. Summoning onto NATURAL tiles (Verdant Pact): the beast appears with Terrain Bond movement bonus active immediately. |

**Design note:** Call of the Wild is the Faunamancer's mid-match recovery spell. At 3 AP and a 3-turn cooldown, it is not fast enough to replace beasts instantly — losing a beast meaningfully sets the Faunamancer back, because she will spend 3 AP and 3 turns of cooldown before the replacement contributes. This deliberate cost discourages reckless beast usage. Choosing which beast to summon is a meaningful decision: Scout Wolf for vision in uncertain mid-games, Siege Boar for a chokepoint that needs holding, Verdant Serpent for ongoing poison stacking against high-HP targets.

## 5. Terrain Interaction Table

### Faunamancer Spell and Beast Impact on Terrain States

| Existing Terrain State | What Happens | Effect on Faunamancer/Beasts | Secondary Effect |
|---|---|---|---|
| **GROUND (normal)** | Standard movement and spell application | Full mobility; all beast commands execute normally | No special interaction |
| **VINES** | Faunamancer is immune to own VINES if planted by allied Floramancer (Floramancer-immunity does not transfer; Faunamancer IS affected). Verdant Serpent ignores VINES movement cost (Serpentine passive) | Faunamancer and Scout Wolf/Siege Boar pay movement cost 2.0 per tile; Verdant Serpent moves freely | ROOTED on turn-end still applies to Faunamancer and non-Serpent beasts |
| **SPORES** | Beasts moving through SPORES take POISONED stacks (1 per tile, plant-origin) — beasts have no immunity | Beasts accumulate POISONED; Verdant Serpent already applies POISONED so its stacks add to SPORES stacks | Scout Wolf tracking enemies through SPORES terrain still functions; vision penetrates terrain states |
| **ON_FIRE** | Beasts take ON_FIRE DoT (5 HP/turn) while on burning tiles | Siege Boar's higher HP makes it more survivable in fire; Scout Wolf and Serpent should not linger | Beasts take BURNING damage; Faunamancer should command beasts out of fire immediately |
| **WET** | No special beast interaction; standard movement | Normal movement; Scout Wolf's 5-tile base is unaffected by WET (no movement penalty on WET terrain for beasts — they are faster than infantry) | WET + Venom Bite from Verdant Serpent: POISONED unit is WET, enabling Contamination spread if Toximancer is active |
| **TOXIC_TERRAIN** | Beasts entering TOXIC_TERRAIN receive 1 POISONED stack per turn (standard terrain interaction) | Serpent is least affected (already POISONED-applying; its own stacks make incoming stacks redundant at high counts). Scout Wolf most vulnerable at 35 HP | TOXIC_TERRAIN + Venom Bite (Serpent): target unit receives stacks from both Serpent and terrain simultaneously — fastest route to DEBILITATED |
| **MUD** | Beasts suffer movement penalty (MUD imposes +1 movement cost) — except Verdant Serpent (Serpentine ignores mud) | Siege Boar at 3 base Move is worst affected — enters mud at effective 4 AP per 2 tiles | Floramancer VINES + MUD combined state (3.0 movement cost) would bring Siege Boar to near-halt |
| **CHARGED** | Walking onto CHARGED tile stuns/damages any unit including beasts | Beasts activate CHARGED arcs normally — 35-HP Scout Wolf is at severe risk from CHARGED tile traps | Avoid routing beasts through Electromancer-prepared CHARGED zones |
| **OBSIDIAN** | Beasts cannot move through OBSIDIAN (solid barrier) | Siege Boar's Charge is stopped by OBSIDIAN; the Boar takes collision damage (8) and the wall is not damaged | Beasts must route around Geomancer-created obsidian walls |
| **OVERGROWTH** | Beasts can move through OVERGROWTH at cost 2.0 (same as units); Serpentine does NOT apply to OVERGROWTH (only VINES and MUD) | Scout Wolf at 5-tile move can still cross 2-tile OVERGROWTH and attack in same command | Ranged attacks against beasts in OVERGROWTH have 25% miss chance (beasts gain cover) |
| **NECROTIC_ASH** | Beasts take necrotic damage (3/turn) from NECROTIC_ASH — they are living creatures | Scout Wolf most at risk; Siege Boar can tolerate brief exposure | NECROTIC_ASH zones from Necromancer are hazards for Faunamancer beasts; coordinate carefully |
| **ICE_TILE** | Beasts on ICE_TILE: movement is not penalized for beasts (they have four-legged stability; no slip mechanic for beasts) | Beasts are not FROZEN by standing on ICE_TILE; only Cryomancer freeze spells FREEZE beasts | Siege Boar's Charge through ICE_TILE slides 1 additional tile (the momentum on ice extends Charge by 1 tile; collision damage unchanged) |
| **NATURAL (VINES, forest, earth)** | Verdant Pact beasts gain +1 movement on natural tiles | Verdant Serpent in VINES: +1 movement and Serpentine movement-cost immunity = full-speed slithering through vine fields | Beasts in natural terrain reinforce Terrain Bond trigger for adjacent Verdant Pact infantry |

### Faunamancer-Specific Terrain Traits (Inherent, Always Active)

- The Faunamancer can command beast companions through any terrain state they can physically traverse — she does not need LOS to her own companions to issue commands. Commands are intuitive (beast-bond communication range is unlimited on the current map).
- Scout Wolf's Track ability ignores OVERGROWTH and STEAM_CLOUD concealment — enemies inside these terrain states are still revealed by Track if within 4 tiles of the Wolf.
- Beasts do not trigger VINES ROOTED on end-of-turn if they were commanded to move (they arrive after the move; the ROOTED check fires on the unit's next stationary turn-end). Exception: beasts commanded to stay in position on a VINES tile trigger ROOTED normally.

## 6. Upgrade Options

All costs are additions to the base 100-point Mancer cost.

### Spell Variants

#### Variant A: Alpha's Howl (replaces Pack Howl) — +20 pts

**Description:** Replaces Pack Howl with a stronger but more focused version. Alpha's Howl targets one specific beast companion rather than all companions globally. The targeted beast gains +6 damage to all attacks (vs. Pack Howl's +3), +2 Move Range (vs. +1), and a one-time immunity to the next status effect applied to it (the pack alpha's hardened focus shrugs off one debuff). Duration: 3 turns (vs. Pack Howl's 2). AP cost: 2 AP; Cooldown: 2 turns. Trade-off: doubles the benefit to one beast instead of spreading moderate benefit to all — best when the Faunamancer is routing one beast as the primary kill unit while others play support roles.

#### Variant B: Apex Predator (replaces Predator's Sense) — +25 pts

**Description:** Replaces Predator's Sense with a more aggressive version. Apex Predator can be cast on a beast companion only (not the Faunamancer herself). The targeted beast gains for 3 turns: all attacks become critical-eligible (+50% damage if the attack would kill the target — lethal-hit bonus); movement is increased by 2 tiles; all terrain states other than OBSIDIAN and FLOODED are ignored for movement cost purposes. The beast becomes a precision assassination unit for those 3 turns. AP cost: 2 AP; Cooldown: 3 turns. Best for Scout Wolf (which becomes a high-speed assassin) or Verdant Serpent (which ignores all terrain and kills poisoned targets instantly).

---

### Passive Traits

#### Passive A: Scent of Blood — +20 pts

**Description:** When any unit (ally or enemy) is reduced to below 30% HP anywhere on the board, all active beast companions immediately gain a free bonus movement of 2 tiles toward the low-HP unit (this does not cost AP and triggers automatically). This represents beasts sensing wounded prey and converging. The free movement is not a command and does not count against the 2-action-per-activation beast cap — it is a passive repositioning. Beasts repositioned by Scent of Blood do not attack; they only move toward the wounded unit.

**Synergy note:** Scent of Blood makes the Faunamancer's pack a cleanup machine in the late game when attrition has reduced enemy units to low HP. Combined with Pack Tactics, a wounded unit that triggers Scent of Blood draws two beasts into adjacent range, setting up a coordinated kill on the Faunamancer's next activation without spending AP to reposition them.

#### Passive B: Wild Bond — +25 pts

**Description:** The maximum number of active beast companions increases from 2 to 3. A third companion slot is unlocked. The third companion must still be summoned via Call of the Wild (it does not begin the match pre-summoned). Commanding a third beast is still 1 AP per action — with 3 beasts, the Faunamancer's activation budget is spread thinner, prioritizing beast commands over personal spells.

**Trade-off:** Three beasts dramatically increase the Faunamancer's board presence and make Pack Tactics more flexible (any beast + Faunamancer = Pack Tactics; with 3 beasts, the other two can threaten separate targets). However, 3 active beasts means the Faunamancer may spend her entire activation on beast commands (3 beasts × 2 actions × 1 AP = 6 AP), leaving no AP for personal spells or movement. Wild Bond players lean into beast-focused play style.

#### Passive C: Tracking Bond — +15 pts

**Description:** All beast companions' attack damage increases by 2 for each BESTIAL_MARK active on any enemy unit on the board (not just the marked unit). This represents the pack becoming more aggressive when the Faunamancer has identified a target. If Bestial Mark is on 2 enemy units simultaneously (requires multiple activations to apply), all beast attacks deal +4 bonus damage total. Stacks with Pack Tactics and Pack Howl additively.

---

### Stat Enhancements

#### Enhancement A: Rugged Hunter (+20 HP, +1 Armor) — +15 pts

**Description:** Max HP increases from 90 to 110; Base Armor increases from 1 to 2. The Faunamancer becomes meaningfully more durable — she can absorb one more standard Mancer spell before reaching critical HP. The armor increase reduces chip damage from Glade Archers and Crossbow Corps by 1 per shot, extending her survivability in attrition matchups.

#### Enhancement B: Ranger's Step (+1 Move Range) — +10 pts

**Description:** Move Range increases from 4 to 5 tiles per activation. The extra tile of movement allows the Faunamancer to stay in positioning range of her beasts as they advance, maximizing the window during which Pack Tactics coordinated attacks are feasible. Also allows faster retreat when beasts have drawn enemy attention and the Faunamancer needs to break contact.

### Signature Ability

#### Signature: Pack Ascendant — +40 pts

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| **Name** | Pack Ascendant |
| **AP Cost** | 5 AP |
| **Cooldown** | 5 turns |
| **Targeting Type** | Self-centered AoE — affects all active beast companions and the Faunamancer |
| **Range** | N/A (global — all active companions regardless of position) |

The 5-AP cost (leaving 1 AP for self-use) means the Faunamancer is spending nearly her full activation to declare Pack Ascendant, but the payoff is 3 turns of dramatically increased pack output. The 5-turn cooldown ensures it is a once-per-long-engagement ability. Against a warband that cannot eliminate beasts quickly, Pack Ascendant turns the Faunamancer's activation into effectively 4 units acting simultaneously at +50% damage.

## 7. Faction Synergy

### Best Pairing: The Verdant Pact (Excellent) / All Factions Viable

The Faunamancer is one of the most faction-flexible Mancers on the roster. Her beasts are self-contained and derive no mechanical benefit from Gilded Throne's Iron Discipline or Ashen Covenant's Deathless Ranks. However, The Verdant Pact provides the most thematic and mechanical synergy.

**Verdant Pact interactions:**

| Mechanism | Effect |
|---|---|
| Beasts as NATURAL affinity units | Beast companions of a Verdant Pact Faunamancer gain +1 movement on natural tiles (forest, VINES, earth/mud). This is a meaningful buff for Scout Wolf (6 tiles on natural terrain) and slightly helps Serpent (5 on natural terrain) |
| Floramancer ally + Verdant Serpent | Verdant Serpent ignores VINES movement cost; the Floramancer's vine fields become free passage for the Serpent while enemies are ROOTED. The Serpent walks through the Floramancer's trap zone and applies POISONED to every ROOTED unit it passes |
| Rootwarden (T2 Chaff) entrench | Rootwarden entrenching creates natural terrain — Siege Boar positioned adjacent to entrenched Rootwarden activates Terrain Bond movement bonus. A Siege Boar in a Rootwarden-fortified position is difficult to bypass (Boar Wall blocks movement) and mobile enough (natural terrain movement bonus) to reposition one tile if needed |
| Glade Archer Poison stacking | Glade Archers apply 1 POISONED stack per hit; Verdant Serpent applies 1-2 stacks per attack. A target receiving coordinated Archer fire + Serpent bites reaches 3-4 POISONED stacks rapidly without any Toximancer investment |
| Terrain Bond Regen for beast companions | Verdant Pact Terrain Bond regen (passive HP on natural tiles) applies to beast companions on natural terrain. Scout Wolf regenerates 3 HP per turn while tracking enemies through forest or vine terrain — meaningfully extending its 35-HP lifespan |

**Gilded Throne — Functional:**
Iron Discipline does not protect against beast attacks (they are physical, not morale-based). The Faunamancer works in a Gilded Throne warband as a pure physical pressure Mancer without faction synergy — the Conscript Spearmen screen protects her while beasts project threat forward. Crossbow Corps firing every other turn can coordinate with Bestial Mark: on Crossbow turns, beasts attack; on reload turns, Faunamancer repositions. The alternating activation rhythm of Crossbow Corps pairs acceptably with beast command management.

**Ashen Covenant — Unconventional:**
Grave Husks as a meat shield screen for the Faunamancer works as a concept — Husks are high-HP chaff that absorb hits — but the Covenant's Deathless Ranks and Remnant token economy gains nothing from beast companions (beasts are not Covenant units; they do not generate Remnant tokens). The Necromancer + Faunamancer combo is noted in Section 8 below.

## 8. Combo Chains

### Combo 1 — Track and Shock (Faunamancer + Electromancer)

**Setup:** Scout Wolf is deployed forward, revealing enemy formation positions through Fog of War (Track passive). The Faunamancer casts Bestial Mark on the highest-value revealed target (1 AP).
**Execution:** Electromancer, now knowing exact enemy positions thanks to Scout Wolf's Track, fires Arc Bolt at the marked target. Pack Tactics does not apply to Electromancer's attack (Electromancer is not a beast), but the BESTIAL_MARK flat +4 damage applies to any beast attacks that follow the Electromancer's chain this activation.
**Result:** Scout Wolf's Track removes the fog-of-war uncertainty that makes Electromancer chain targeting risky. Combined with the Electromancer's STUNNED output and Bestial Mark, the STUNNED targets can then be attacked by beasts on the Faunamancer's next activation — Pack Tactics fires against a unit that cannot respond.

**Tactical outcome:** STUNNED targets cannot activate — the Faunamancer's next activation against STUNNED targets is guaranteed Pack Tactics without risk of the target repositioning. This turns the Electromancer's chain stun into a guaranteed Pack Tactics window, one of the highest-efficiency attack turns available to the Faunamancer.

### Combo 2 — The Poison Pack (Faunamancer + Toximancer)

**Setup:** Toximancer applies 2-3 POISONED stacks to a target. Verdant Serpent applies Venom Bite (1 additional stack) to the same target via Pack Tactics (Faunamancer also attacks — Pack Tactics bonus applies).
**Execution:** At 3-4 POISONED stacks, the Toximancer casts Venom Burst (or any stack-escalating spell) to push the target to 5 stacks (DEBILITATED). Verdant Serpent Venom Spit adds another stack on the same activation.
**Result:** DEBILITATED target (-1 move, -1 spell range) with 15 HP/turn DoT. Pack Tactics Faunamancer + Serpent deal +25% damage to the DEBILITATED target. The combination of 15 HP/turn passive damage and reduced spell range makes the target ineffective until cleansed.

**CONTAMINATION interaction:** A POISONED unit hit by Toximancer's water interaction (WET + POISONED = Contamination spread) becomes the center of a spread event. If the Verdant Serpent pushed the target to 5 stacks and the target moves through WET terrain, surrounding allies receive 1 POISONED stack each — the Serpent's stacks contribute to the contamination event.

---

### Combo 3 — Vine Trap and Pack (Faunamancer + Floramancer)

**Setup:** Floramancer plants VINES across an enemy approach. Enemy units end their turn on VINES and are ROOTED (1-turn duration).
**Execution:** Faunamancer activates: Bestial Mark on a ROOTED target (1 AP), Siege Boar Charge into the ROOTED cluster (2 AP), Faunamancer casts Bestial Surge (spell variant, if taken) or Pack Howl (2 AP) — pack attacks land on immobile targets.
**Result:** ROOTED units cannot dodge Siege Boar's Charge or position away from the Verdant Serpent's Venom Bite. Pack Tactics fires on stationary targets. Verdant Serpent can move freely through the VINES field (Serpentine) while Siege Boar's Charge covers the distance at full speed from outside the VINES zone.

---

### Combo 4 — Corpse Feeding (Faunamancer + Necromancer)

**Setup:** Faunamancer sends Scout Wolf deep behind enemy lines using Sprint. Scout Wolf's Track reveals enemy backline positions including ranged units.
**Execution:** Necromancer applies Death Mark to a revealed enemy unit (based on Scout Wolf's track data). Enemy kills the Scout Wolf or Scout Wolf is sacrificed to bait enemy attention. Scout Wolf's death creates a corpse adjacent to enemy formation.
**Result:** Necromancer raises a Risen Shambler from Scout Wolf's corpse (Standard Corpse quality, 30 HP melee unit — beasts leave standard corpses). The Faunamancer summons a replacement Scout Wolf via Call of the Wild (3 AP) on the same activation. The Necromancer gains a corpse from a beast the Faunamancer voluntarily traded — an unusual but intentional synergy.

**Why it works:** The Faunamancer can deliberately sacrifice a low-HP Scout Wolf in enemy territory to seed a corpse at an advanced position — the Necromancer raises into that position immediately, placing a Risen Shambler deep in enemy lines where it would otherwise cost the Necromancer multiple turns to reach.

## 9. Counters and Weaknesses

### What Shuts Down the Faunamancer

**Beast elimination:** The most efficient counter. Each dead beast reduces Faunamancer output permanently (until Call of the Wild, 3 turns later). AoE Mancers — Pyromancer Scorched Earth, Electromancer chain arcs through WET terrain, Sonimancer cone attacks — can sweep beasts off the board more efficiently than single-target fire. A warband that kills both beasts in consecutive activations leaves the Faunamancer as a moderate single-Mancer with no pack for 3 turns.

**Fast aggression before Call of the Wild becomes relevant:** The Faunamancer starts with 1 beast companion. Before her second beast is summoned (3 AP, 3-turn cooldown), she is operating at half her intended capacity. An opponent that forces decisive combat in the first 2-3 turns, before the second beast enters play, denies the Faunamancer her full threat profile.

**HEAVY status (Gravimancer):** HEAVY status makes units resistant to displacement and increases fall damage — beasts attempting to position aggressively (Siege Boar Charge) against HEAVY targets are not knocked back normally. The Siege Boar's primary Charge value is knockback collision damage; HEAVY units resist this. Against a Gravimancer-heavy warband, the Siege Boar loses its highest-value ability's secondary function.

**Silenced Faunamancer:** A Silenced Faunamancer cannot command beasts (commanding is a spell action). Without AP-spend commands, beasts hold position. An opponent who Silences the Faunamancer effectively disables the entire beast pack for that turn — no commands, no attacks, no repositioning. This is the single most effective status effect against the Faunamancer.

**Ranged focus on Scout Wolf:** The Scout Wolf's 35 HP with 0 armor is trivially eliminated by 2-3 Crossbow Corps shots or a single moderate spell. An opponent who prioritizes the Scout Wolf first loses the track information advantage and removes the Faunamancer's primary scouting tool before she establishes board presence. Scout Wolf replacement takes 3 turns — the information blackout is meaningful.
